# Platform design walkthrough

Status: DESIGN WALKTHROUGH COMPLETE. Sam approved the written handoff at Q44 on November 3, 2026 with "Yes it does." Page patterns, responsive approach, requirements and local task graph are confirmed. Full-platform implementation follows the separate implement-spec workflow.

## Settled decisions

1. Site organization: four main destinations, Records, Managers, Seasons, and Rivalries. The league name returns Home. Records contains the record book, leaderboards, and exhibits. Seasons leads to standings, brackets, and matchups. Q18/Q19 remove standalone public draft boards and exhaustive transaction feeds from v1. The Managers directory also provides access to separate franchise histories.
2. Design completion: a written screen contract for every required route, with reviewed desktop and mobile examples for each distinct page family. Routine entity and filter variants reuse those patterns. Preserve the approved home-page visual system.
3. Public calculations: Sam rejected public evidence/calculation views. He considers this low-stakes fantasy history and expects the results to be correct. Remove calculation panels, methodology/source dumps, and evidence-specific UI from the full-platform design. Keep correctness rules in implementation, deterministic tests, and developer documentation. Actual history browsing destinations remain to be resolved below.
4. Filter context, Q5: filters belong to the current page and its shareable URL. Related links preserve relevant season/context, main navigation opens the section's defaults, and browser Back restores prior filters and sorting.
5. Public status, Q6: Sam rejected public status indicators and updated dates. The visitor experience assumes the archive is current. Keep operational import/refresh diagnostics internal; do not add public freshness timestamps or status UI to the designs.
6. Interview channel: ask every question directly in chat. Do not use the structured ask-user tools; Sam cannot see those questions reliably.
7. Record destinations, Q4: Sam selected mockup B. Opening a record leads to a record-category page showing the top people/results in that particular category. The category page is a ranked archive view, not a calculation or evidence page. Selected visual reference: prototypes/record-navigation/.impeccable/mocks/b-record-page.png. Ranking behavior is settled in decisions 8–10.
8. Ranking rows, Q7: game and season categories rank individual qualifying performances. A manager may hold multiple places. Career categories have one aggregate row per manager.
9. Entry destinations, Q8: a category leaderboard entry opens its relevant matchup, manager-season, or career profile. The agreed flow is Home or Records → category leaderboard → specific history.
10. Ranking length, Q9: show the top ten places initially, include all entries tied at the cutoff, and provide Show more for further results.
11. Records organization, Q10: one collection of clearly named categories grouped into Careers, Seasons, Matchups, and Players. Ordinary records and exhibits share the B category-leaderboard pattern.
12. Profile structure, Q11: a compact career summary followed by Seasons, Rivalries, and Players tabs, with Seasons initially selected. Franchise profiles reuse the family and include ownership history. Sam additionally requires the manager's career summary to show when the franchise was acquired and the previous manager when applicable, plus a timeline-like presentation of team-name changes within the same franchise. Q13 settles the division between manager-tenure and full-franchise timelines.
13. Season structure, Q12: Standings is the initial view, with local tabs Standings, Matchups, Playoffs, and Players. The originally proposed Draft and Transactions tabs are superseded by Q18/Q19. Q21 places contextual player and move analysis in Players. The season header includes champion and runner-up once those results exist.
14. Timeline boundary, Q13: the manager's timeline covers their tenure, including takeover, predecessor, and team-name changes; the linked franchise profile shows the full timeline across all owners.
15. Rivalries, Q14: visitors select two managers to see head-to-head record, scoring margins, and matchup history. Links from profiles/rankings preselect the pair.
16. Player destinations, Q17: selecting a player opens a contribution view scoped to the selected manager or franchise, showing season totals and the games that produced them.
17. Draft scope, Q18: use draft data as an input to distinctive contributor and league-history statistics. Omit a standalone public draft board in v1.
18. Transaction scope, Q19: retain full transaction history internally to calculate significant pickup/drop outcomes. Omit an exhaustive public transaction feed in v1.
19. Records versus analysis, Q21: ranked move categories belong in Records. Contextual player and move analysis belongs in the Players views on manager and season pages. Preserve the four main destinations.
20. Productive pickups, Q22: rank waiver and free-agent acquisitions by actual starting-lineup points for the acquiring manager from acquisition until the player leaves that team or the season ends. Bench points do not contribute. This is a contribution ranking, not a claim that the move caused wins.
21. Significant drops, Q23: rank dropped players by subsequent starting-lineup points for other managers in this league during the same season. This describes their recorded contributions elsewhere, not hypothetical wins the dropping manager lost.
22. Schedule Victims, Q24: rank completed regular seasons by expected wins minus actual wins. Expected wins uses the fraction of other teams outscored each completed week, with half credit for tied scores. Use the same eligible weeks for expected and actual results; a tied actual game contributes half a win.
23. Nemesis, Q25: use the selected head-to-head scope and rank opponents by losses minus wins from the selected manager's perspective. Preserve ties. Assign no nemesis when no opponent has a winning record against that manager.
24. Streaks, Q26: career streaks continue across season boundaries within the selected game scope. Season-specific streaks stop at the season boundary. A tied game ends either a winning or losing streak.
25. Scoring margins, Q27: rank closest games by smallest final scoring margin and biggest blowouts by largest. Tied games have a zero margin. No arbitrary close-game or blowout cutoff is needed.
26. Rate-ranking eligibility, Q28: include every manager with at least one eligible game in career rate rankings. Show the game count or W-L-T record beside the rate on the ranking page. Do not add another minimum-games cutoff.
27. Current-season inclusion, Q29: completed current-season games count toward career totals, individual-game records, and player contributions. Full-season award rankings wait until the season finishes. Season pages show completed weeks. This does not add public freshness/status indicators.
28. Home breadth, Q31: feature six all-time categories: Best season without a title, Highest-scoring benched player, Unluckiest season, Most productive pickup, Dropped players who delivered elsewhere, and Longest winning streak. Keep the original two as prominent features and show the other four as compact highlights. Each opens its category leaderboard. Judge the expanded arrangement in a mockup before implementation.

## Scope reconsideration

Q15 and Q16 were not accepted. Sam cautioned against recreating analytics and archives already available in the fantasy app. The product should emphasize specialized, interesting league statistics. Q18/Q19 settle the replacement scope: keep the source data internally and omit the public draft board and exhaustive transaction feed in v1.

Q20 was not accepted as a blanket placement rule. Sam distinguishes records from analysis. Q21 resolves placement, and Q22/Q23 establish the first pickup/drop rankings. A transaction summary does not automatically become a record.

Q30 was not accepted as a two-category home-page content limit. Q31/Q32 settle the broader category selection and recent-result policy. Q34 approves the expanded desktop composition. Preserve the accepted visual system and the earlier preference for less clutter.

Q35 was rejected in its first form. Sam called the season overview sloppy and unrefined, said its large middle sections repeat information already understood, and specifically disliked the bottom. The revised version removes the outsized context blocks and appended player-summary strip, preserves the four tabs and Standings default, and uses the existing dominance and schedule-result metrics. Sam accepted it as a working foundation and deferred further mobile polish in decision 33.

Q38 cleanup is saved as prototypes/history-details/.impeccable/mocks/matchup-clean.png. It removes only the separate record caption beside Bench, retaining the quiet selected-row highlight. This is the canonical matchup reference; the earlier PNG remains unchanged. Q40–Q43 are approved.
