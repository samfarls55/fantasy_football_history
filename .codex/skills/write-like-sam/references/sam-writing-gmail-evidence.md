# Gmail evidence behind Sam's writing profile

Reviewed September 18, 2026. This report refines the [writing profile](../SKILL.md) with sent email from September 18, 2023 through September 18, 2026. The [message audit](sam-writing-gmail-audit.md) records coverage and exclusions.

## What changes

The original profile described Sam's explanatory and reflective writing well, but it was too essay-centered to serve as a default for correspondence. The email evidence supports a much wider range: a three-word familiar question, a short warm confirmation, a detailed troubleshooting explanation, and direct disagreement.

The strongest new default is: acknowledge the person or situation, supply only the context needed, make the question or next action clear, and close naturally. This is a recurring pattern rather than a required template.

| Earlier emphasis | Updated interpretation |
|---|---|
| Moderately long, complete sentences | Suitable for extended reasoning; email also uses brief sentences and fragments. |
| Context before the conclusion | Context expands when needed; confirmations and existing threads can start with the answer. |
| Logistics evidence was limited | Email now supplies direct evidence of requests, scheduling, follow-ups, and service disputes. |
| Thoughtful qualifications | Still present, often as a concrete question about a discrepancy or process. |
| Occasional understated humor | Still plausible, but warmth and appreciation are much better supported than frequent jokes. |
| Avoid generic AI phrases | Judge by purpose and actual usage. Sam does use "Please let me know" and "Hope this helps!" |
| Gentle, reasoned recommendations | One mode among several. Sam can be blunt when a response fails to address the problem. |

## Coverage and measurements

All 170 matching messages were retrieved. The new plain-text message content was read across the complete set, rather than sampling only long or recent emails. 101 contain usable prose; 69 contain no useful original prose or were excluded for the reasons in the audit.

Of the 101 usable messages:

- 75 contain fewer than 50 whitespace-separated words in the extracted new text.
- Median extracted message length is about 31 words.
- 68 contain "thanks" or "thank you", case-insensitive.
- 69 contain at least one exclamation mark.

Length includes greetings, typed closings, and any inline details or quotations remaining in the new message, but excludes quoted reply chains and standard automatic signatures. These are descriptive extraction counts, not an exact count of independently authored words. Short repeated attachment notes remain in these message counts. They should not be treated as separate rich samples.

The corpus contains 14 usable messages from late 2023, 24 from 2024, 44 from 2025, and 19 from 2026. Recurring patterns across these periods support the updates. This is evidence of differences between genres; it does not establish that Sam's entire writing style changed with age.

## Confidence and source handling

High confidence: short practical messages, frequent appreciation, direct questions, context proportional to the problem, conversational softeners, ordinary contractions, and specific explanations of what went wrong. These recur across years and different recipients.

Moderate confidence: the precise style of networking notes and longer advice, because there are fewer examples. Natural fragments and occasional doubled exclamation marks are supported but should depend on relationship and purpose.

Limited evidence: social posts, creative prose, broad emoji preferences, humor frequency, and workplace team communication. This personal Gmail account contains considerable housing, customer-service, recruiting, and administrative correspondence. It should not stand in for a work mailbox or all private conversations.

Being in Sent establishes that a message came from the account, not that every word was independently composed by Sam. The two explicitly labeled synthetic-data messages and the study-topic list were excluded from the voice model. Code-only emails, automatic unsubscribes, attachment-only notes without prose, identifiers, and link-only messages were also excluded. In mixed messages, quoted resume bullets and copied organizational instructions were not taken as Sam's own phrasing.

Quoted history and forwarded text were separated to prevent other people's prose and repeated earlier messages from dominating the profile. Attachments, links, and images were not read in this pass. No inference about AI authorship was made solely because a message sounded polished.

The guide preserves observations that a generic anti-AI editing checklist might erase: "Just wanted to", "however", "Please let me know", occasional formal wording, and enthusiastic thanks. For new drafts, actual evidence of Sam's usage takes priority over mechanical bans.

## Using the revision

Use the updated profile as the single drafting guide. Use this report for email examples and the original evidence report for longer explanatory writing. Sam's future revisions should outweigh an inferred rule when they show how he wants to sound in the same setting.

The previous football sample leaned toward the older explanatory mode. This review suggests using less of that essay structure for casual requests, while retaining its cause-and-effect reasoning when a fuller explanation is wanted. That is an application of the findings, not a user-confirmed evaluation of that draft.
